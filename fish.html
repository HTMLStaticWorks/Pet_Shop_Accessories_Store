Aquarium Maintenance

Keep Your Aquarium Crystal Clear & Thriving

A well-maintained aquarium is a healthy aquarium. Regular maintenance prevents disease outbreaks, keeps water parameters stable, and ensures your fish live long, vibrant lives.

  • Weekly 25% Water Change: Remove detritus from gravel and replace 25% of water with treated tap water.
  • Test Parameters Weekly: Check pH, ammonia, nitrite, and nitrate weekly to catch issues before they become problems.
  • Clean Algae Monthly: Scrub glass, clean filter media in old tank water (not tap), and trim any overgrown live plants.
  • Monitor Temperature Daily: Use a digital thermometer. Temperature swings of more than 2°F stress fish and can suppress immune function.

Beginner's Guide

Step-by-Step Aquarium Setup Guide

Follow these six steps to safely set up your first aquarium and create a healthy, thriving aquatic environment.

1
Choose the Right Tank Size

Bigger tanks are more stable and easier to maintain. Begin with a minimum 20-gallon tank for freshwater community fish. Smaller tanks experience parameter swings much faster and are harder to manage for beginners.

2
Add Substrate & Decor

Rinse aquarium gravel or sand thoroughly. Add driftwood, rocks, and decorations to create hiding spots. If using live plants, use nutrient-rich planted substrate.

3
Install Filter, Heater & Lighting

Install your filter on the back or inside the tank. Set the heater to 76–80°F for tropical fish. Connect your LED lighting system and set timer for 8–10 hours of light per day.

4
Fill with Treated Water

Fill with tap water and immediately add water conditioner to neutralise chlorine and chloramine. Test pH (target 7.0 for most community fish) and temperature before proceeding.

5
Cycle the Aquarium (2–4 Weeks)

This is the most important step. Add beneficial bacteria starter and run the filter. Test ammonia and nitrite levels daily. Only add fish when both read 0 ppm and nitrate appears.

6
Introduce Fish Gradually

Add fish slowly — no more than 3–4 fish per week. Float the bag for 15 minutes to temperature-equalise, then net fish into the tank. Do not add bag water to your tank.

Common Questions

Aquarium FAQs

What size aquarium is best for beginners?+
A 20-gallon tank is ideal for beginners. Larger tanks have more stable water chemistry, making them easier to manage. Avoid nano tanks under 5 gallons unless you have experience — they require much more precise maintenance to keep parameters stable.
How long does it take to cycle a new aquarium?+
The nitrogen cycle typically takes 4–6 weeks. However, using a bacterial starter product (like API Quick Start or Seachem Stability) can significantly reduce cycling time to as little as 2 weeks. Test water daily and only add fish when ammonia and nitrite both read 0 ppm.
What fish are best for community tanks?+
For beginners, excellent community fish include Neon Tetras, Guppies, Mollies, Corydoras Catfish, and Harlequin Rasboras. They are hardy, peaceful, and colourful. Avoid aggressive species like Tiger Barbs, Cichlids (most), and Bettas in community setups.
How often should I feed my fish?+
Feed adult fish once or twice daily — only as much as they consume in 2–3 minutes. Overfeeding is the #1 cause of poor water quality and fish death. Uneaten food decomposes, spikes ammonia, and promotes algae growth.
Do live plants require special lighting?+
Low-light plants like Java Fern, Anubias, and Cryptocoryne grow well under standard LED aquarium lights for 8–10 hours daily. Medium and high-light plants (Rotala, Monte Carlo, Dwarf Hair Grass) need full-spectrum LEDs rated at 50+ PAR. Our programmable LED bar supports both beginner and advanced planted tanks.
